520, 1314 and the secret love codes of Chinese

Guide · 5 min read

Say 520 out loud in Mandarin, wǔ èr líng, and it sounds close to 我爱你 wǒ ài nǐ, “I love you”. That pun turned May 20 into an unofficial Valentine's Day. Couples register marriages on it, brands launch gift campaigns, and red-envelope transfers of ¥520 are common.

The core love codes

  • 520 / 521 — I love you / I do.
  • 1314 — 一生一世, a whole lifetime. So 5201314 = “I love you forever”.
  • 9420 — 就是爱你, “It's you I love”.
  • 530 — I miss you. 77 — kiss kiss. 3344 — life after life.

Gift and money amounts

Gifting ¥520, ¥1314 or ¥5201.314 in a digital red envelope is a romantic gesture. Avoid amounts with 4, and never gift 250, which means “idiot”.

Pricing for Chinese shoppers: the endings that sell

Guide · 6 min read

Western retail uses “.99” to make prices feel lower. In Chinese-influenced markets, the ending also carries a meaning. Common practice:

  • Favor 8 and 88 for prosperity: ¥88, ¥188, ¥888.
  • 6 and 66 for smoothness: good for services and travel.
  • 9 and 99 for longevity: gifts and anniversaries.
  • 168 / 518 / 1688 as premium bundle prices.
  • Avoid 4, 14, 74 and 250 in prices, SKUs and discount codes.

Seasonal moments

Plan number-themed promotions around 11.11 (Singles' Day), 6.18 (mid-year shopping festival), 5.20 (love day), Qixi (7th day of the 7th lunar month) and Chinese New Year.

How to pick a lucky phone number (checklist)

Guide · 4 min read

  1. Focus on the last 4 digits. That's what people repeat.
  2. No 4 at the end, and no 14, 74 or 250 anywhere.
  3. Look for patterns: AAAA, AABB, ABAB and rising runs (步步高).
  4. Match your goal: business → 8s, 168, 518; love → 520, 1314; calm → 6s and 9s.
  5. Check both Mandarin and Cantonese if your audience is southern China, Hong Kong or overseas.

Collectors take this seriously. Wikipedia's Chinese numerology article records a Chengdu number, +86 28 8888 8888, that sold for about US$280,000 in 2003.

11.11: how four “ones” became the world's biggest shopping day

Guide · 3 min read

The digit 1 looks like a bare stick, and 光棍 guānggùn (“bare sticks”) is slang for single people. So 11 November began as a university joke, Singles' Day. E-commerce platforms turned it into a massive annual sales festival. It's a big reason 11, the first half of 11176, is so recognizable in Chinese culture today.
