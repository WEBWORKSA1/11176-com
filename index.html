The name

Why “11176”?

In Chinese, numbers are read for their sound. When people read out phone numbers, 1 is often said as 幺 yāo, which sounds like 要 yào — “want / will”. 7 (qī) echoes 起 qǐ “rise”, and 6 (liù) echoes 溜 / 顺 — “smooth”.

So 11176 reads as 要要要 · 起溜 — “want it, want it, want it — rise and roll smoothly.” Its digits add up to 16, and 一路 yī lù means “all the way”, the same pun behind 168 一路发.

This is our reading of the number. 11176 has no single official meaning in Chinese culture, and that's the point: every number can be decoded. See the full 11176 breakdown →

FAQ

How do Chinese number meanings work?

Mandarin has many words that sound alike. Numbers borrow the meaning of words they sound like: 8 (bā) ≈ 发 fā “prosper”, 4 (sì) ≈ 死 sǐ “death”, and 520 (wǔ èr líng) ≈ 我爱你 wǒ ài nǐ “I love you”.

Is the luck score scientific?

No. It's a transparent, rules-based summary of popular folk beliefs and internet usage. It's meant for fun, culture and decisions like branding and pricing, and it can't predict the future.

What is the luckiest number in Chinese culture?

8. It sounds like 发 (prosperity), which is why the Beijing Olympics opened on 8/8/08 at 8:08 pm, and why numbers full of 8s have sold for large sums.
